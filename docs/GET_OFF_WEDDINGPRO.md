# Getting off WeddingPro: the lead plan

Written 2026-11-01. Owner: Jacob. Working partner: Nic.

## 1. The situation

WeddingPro (The Knot + WeddingWire listings) is the only channel bringing in leads, and it is
going away. Our records say the contract (Q-472003) was signed 2026-08-27 for 12 months at about
$416/month. If it is ending sooner than August 2027, nothing below changes. It just has to happen
faster.

**The clock matters more than the contract.** December through February is proposal season. A
large share of engagements happen between Thanksgiving and Valentine's Day, and those couples start
booking vendors in January and February for weddings 9 to 14 months out. The next 90 days are the
most valuable lead window of the whole year. Every channel below should be live by mid-December.

**The goal:** by the time WeddingPro is gone, at least three channels each bringing in leads on
their own, and a number for each one telling us what a booked wedding cost us.

## 2. Measure first (built and shipped with this plan)

Without this, every channel below gets judged on feel. Now it gets judged on bookings.

- **"How did you find us?"** is on the `/book` form: Zola, The Knot, WeddingWire, Google,
  Instagram, Facebook, TV (Roku/Hulu), business card or brochure, a friend, our venue, another
  vendor, other. Friend, venue and vendor ask "who?", which is how referral credit gets paid and
  how we know which venue to thank.
- **Tagged links (`?src=`).** Any link to the site can carry a tag, and the site remembers it for
  120 days, so a couple who saw a Hulu ad and booked two weeks later still counts for the ad.
  Printed business cards (`/book?with=jake` and `?with=nic`) count as cards automatically.
- **`crossroadsweddingco.shop/tv`**: the short address for TV spots. Nobody clicks a TV ad; they
  type what they saw.
- **The admin dashboard** now opens with a "Where couples found us" table: requests and paid
  deposits per channel. The booking notification email says where each couple came from.

## 3. The channels, ranked by leads per dollar

### 3.1 Venues: the best free channel, and yes, call them

Venues talk to every couple 12 to 18 months before the wedding, and couples ask them "who do you
recommend?" more than they ask anyone. Being on a venue's preferred-vendor list is worth more than
any ad.

**About last-minute cancellations:** yes, but as one line in the call, not as the reason for it.
When a couple cancels, the venue resells the date, often to a couple getting married in 60 to 120
days who now needs every vendor at once. A DJ who can say "we can usually do short notice, and our
price is flat and on the website" is exactly who a venue coordinator wants to hand that couple. So
the ask is: *put us on your list, and think of us first when a date gets resold on short notice.*

What we ask for:
1. A spot on their preferred or recommended vendor list.
2. Permission to leave a small stack of brochures and cards (`?src=venue` QR on the next print).
3. A walkthrough, so we can say "we've worked your room" with a straight face.
4. A heads-up when a short-notice booking comes in.

What we offer: we show up early, we follow their rules on load-in and cutoffs, our sound is tuned
for their room, and we run the timeline so their coordinator does not have to. Kat and Tanis at
Forge on 4th (November 7) is the first proof: ask Forge on 4th for an intro to the next couple
before we even leave that night.

### 3.2 Other vendors: photographers, planners, florists, caterers, bakers

Same logic as venues. Photographers especially get asked for DJ recommendations and get the same
couples 12 months early. Offer a simple swap: we recommend them, they recommend us, and each side
can see the other's work. Find them through Zola's and The Knot's vendor search for our cities and
through the photographers tagged at the venues above on Instagram. Goal: 10 real relationships by
February, not 50 cold emails.

### 3.3 Zola (started)

Zola is where a lot of couples build their wedding website and registry, and vendor search sits
right next to it. Checklist:

- [ ] Profile complete: all three services (DJ & MC $1,000, live solo acoustic $500, bar service
      $250 for one bartender / $400 for two). Never write "minimum" for the bar.
- [ ] Website field set to `crossroadsweddingco.shop/?src=zola`.
- [ ] Service area: Columbus home base, two-hour radius, the six cities.
- [ ] Photos: real ones from Kat and Tanis on November 7 (ask permission; the contract has the
      marketing-photo clause with opt-out).
- [ ] Reviews: ask every couple within a week of the wedding. Zola lets you import or request
      reviews; the first five matter most.
- [ ] Response time under one hour during business hours. Couples on these sites message several
      DJs at once and book whoever answers first and clearly.
- [ ] **Check in the Zola vendor dashboard whether replying to an inquiry costs credits or money.**
      Public information on this is thin and contradictory. If inquiries cost money, treat Zola
      like a paid channel and judge it on cost per booking, same as WeddingPro.

### 3.9 Roku and Hulu (connected TV): a test, not a foundation

Honest take: TV reaches a lot of people and very few of them are engaged right now, so it is the
most expensive way on this list to get one couple. It can still be worth a small test in January,
when engagement is at its peak, if the channels above are already running.

- **Platforms**: Roku Ads Manager and Disney Campaign Manager (the self-serve tool formerly called
  Hulu Ad Manager). Both are reported at about **$500 minimum per campaign**, with targeting by
  age and zip code. Those figures come from third-party guides, not the platforms' own pages, so
  confirm in each dashboard before committing.
  ([Roku cost guide](https://www.vibe.co/blog/roku-advertising-cost),
  [Hulu self-serve guide](https://mountain.com/blog/hulu-advertising/))
- **Targeting**: the zip codes in our two-hour radius, ages roughly 24 to 38. Run January 1 to
  February 15.
- **The ad**: 15 or 30 seconds, ending on a big, plain end card: *Wedding DJ, $1,000 flat.
  crossroadsweddingco.shop/tv*. Say the address out loud too.
- **Blocker: the existing reel cannot run as is.** Its recorded voiceover still says "five hundred
  is the minimum" for the bar (no longer true since 2026-10-03), and it still shows .com. Re-record
  the voiceover and re-render before any of it goes on TV.
- **How we judge it**: bookings tagged `tv` (and couples who answer "Saw an ad on TV") against the
  spend. If $1,000 across both platforms produces zero requests by March, stop. If it produces one
  booked wedding, it paid for itself.

### 3.11 Engagement announcements (optional, light touch)

Local papers (The Republic in Columbus, the Herald-Times in Bloomington) still run engagement
announcements, which couples submit themselves to be public. A one-time handwritten congratulations
card with a brochure is an old, accepted wedding-vendor practice. Rules: one touch only, a card,
never a call or text, never anything scraped beyond what the couple published, and stop the moment
anyone finds it unwelcome.

## 4. What we will not do

- **No pulling couples out of registries (Target, Amazon, Zola, The Knot).** Registries can only
  be searched by a couple's name, they do not show contact details or addresses, and bulk-pulling
  them breaks every one of those sites' terms. Contacting people that way would also run into
  spam and telemarketing law (CAN-SPAM, TCPA) and would read as creepy to exactly the couples we
  want. The couples who need us are found through the people they already ask: venues,
  photographers, friends, and Google.
- **No cold texts or calls to couples.** Venues and vendors, yes. Couples only when they reach out
  first, or with one mailed card per 3.10.
- **No fake reviews, no review swaps, no paid reviews.**
