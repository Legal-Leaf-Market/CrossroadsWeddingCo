## Rules (read before you start)

1. **Research only. Contact nobody.** Do not send email, submit contact forms,
   message anyone, comment, RSVP, sign up or buy anything. Jake makes every
   contact himself.
2. **Public pages only.** Don't log in anywhere, don't get around paywalls, and
   don't scrape a whole site. Read the pages a person would read.
3. **Cite everything.** Every fact gets the URL you read it on. If a page says it,
   quote the exact words. If you're inferring, say "inferred" and why.
4. **Never invent.** No guessed names, phone numbers, emails, dates or budgets. A
   blank with "not found" beats a plausible guess, because Jake will say it out
   loud on the phone.
5. **This year's date is not last year's date.** Many listings still show 2025.
   Report which year a page is about.
6. **No em dashes in anything you write.** The owner's standing rule. Use a comma,
   period, colon or parentheses.
7. **Time box:** about 15 minutes per lead. Go deeper on the 4 and 5 star
   prospects.

## What to find for every lead

1. **Is it happening in 2026, and exactly when?** Date, start and end time, and
   the venue address.
2. **Who decides on entertainment?** The person or committee who books music or
   sound: name, title, direct phone or email *as published*. Also the main
   organizer if that's someone different.
3. **Is music or sound already booked?** Look for performer names, a DJ, a band, a
   sponsor providing sound, or wording like "entertainment TBA". Check last year's
   recap, photos and video too: who did sound in 2025?
4. **Signs they need us.** For example:
   - posts asking for vendors, volunteers or sponsors for sound or music
   - a vendor or entertainment application
   - a Craigslist, Facebook or Nextdoor post looking for a DJ
   - a new venue or a new event with nothing set yet
   - complaints about sound last year
5. **Size and budget clues.** Expected crowd, ticket price, sponsorship tiers,
   whether it's a fundraiser, indoor or outdoor (outdoor in December means power
   and weather questions).
6. **Ways in.** The organizer's other events in 2026 and 2027 we could pitch, any
   connection to Columbus, Valle Vista Golf Club (our October 17 wedding) or Forge
   on 4th, and chamber or business-association ties.
7. **The best opening line.** One specific, true sentence Jake can open the call
   with, taken from what you found ("I saw your 2025 recap thanked ... for sound").

## Where to look

- The event's own page (the source URL below), and the organizer's site: About,
  Contact, Staff, Board and Sponsors pages
- Last year's coverage: local news, the event's Facebook page and event listing,
  Instagram posts and photo captions
- Facebook Events, Eventbrite and AllEvents listings for 2026
- Craigslist (Indianapolis and Bloomington): gigs, events and creative sections
- Chamber and visitor-bureau calendars: chamberbloomington.org,
  visitbloomington.com, festivalcountryindiana.com, discoverdowntownfranklin.com,
  indyarts.org
- LinkedIn public pages for the named contacts (don't log in to see more)

## Star rating (use exactly this scale)

| Stars | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| 5 | Confirmed they need a DJ or sound for a date we have open: they posted it (a Craigslist or Facebook ad, a vendor call) or their page says entertainment is still open. Call right now. |
| 4 | Strong: entertainment not named yet, a reachable decision-maker, and a date we have open. Or a warm tie (Valle Vista, Forge on 4th, someone Jake knows). |
| 3 | Real fit: right size and type of event, a published contact, but no sign either way on music. |
| 2 | Weak: likely booked already, or no reachable contact, or the 2026 date isn't confirmed. Mostly a 2027 conversation. |
| 1 | Not worth a call: already booked with named performers, cancelled, no music, or not a fit (too small, members only, wrong place). |

The playbook's current rating is given for each lead. Raise or lower it only on
evidence, and say what moved it.
